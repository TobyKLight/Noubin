## 2. Using the Name ("Noubin")
We want you to proudly state that your product or software supports Noubin, provided you are transparent about which version of the standard you support.

* **Latest Standard:** If your product, software, or release fully supports the **latest** published version of the Noubin standard, you may use the word "Noubin" freely (e.g., "Noubin Player," "Plays Noubins").
* **Historical Standards:** If your product supports an older, superseded version of the standard, you may still use the name, but you **must explicitly state the version number** (e.g., "Supports Noubin V1 standard"). You may not use the unversioned word "Noubin" in a way that implies compatibility with the latest standard. 

*Why? We want the freedom to fix unanticipated security issues or address abuse in future updates. If we release a necessary update, consumers need to trust that an unversioned "Noubin" product is up-to-date and secure.* 

## 3. Using the Logo (Strictly Latest Standard Only)
The Noubin logo is our strongest signal of current trust and compatibility. 
* You may **only** use the Noubin logo on products, software, or marketing materials that support the **latest** Noubin standard.
* If your product relies on a historical standard, you **must stop using the Noubin logo**. You may continue to use the word "Noubin" according to the versioning rules in Section 2, but the logo is strictly reserved for the current standard.

## 4. Grace Periods for Standard Updates
When we release an updated Noubin standard you are granted the following grace periods to either update your product to the latest standard (if affected) or update your marketing/branding to reflect historical support:

* **Digital Materials (1 Month):** You have thirty (30) days from the release of a new standard to update websites, digital marketing, and software user interfaces. 
* **Physical Materials (6 Months):** You have six (6) months from the release of a new standard to update printed materials, physical product packaging, and hardware enclosures.

*To be clear: You have as much time as you like to actually comply with the new standard should you wish to do so, the time limits are on how long you have to update your communications about which version of the standard you currently support.* 

## 5. Permitted and Restricted Terminology
To differentiate official releases from DIY projects, to leave room for future official verification programs, and to provide certainty to end-users the following rules apply to words used alongside the Noubin brand:

* **Restricted Terms:** You may **not** use the terms **"Verified", "Authenticated", "Authentic", or "Certified"** in connection with the Noubin brand (e.g., "Certified Noubin"). Now We Make UG reserves these terms exclusively for potential future official verification programs.
* **Permitted Terms for Artists:** Artists and creators making  Noubins to accompany their digital media releases are encouraged to use the terms **"Official," "Original," and "Genuine"** (e.g., "The Official Noubin for [Album Name]"). This helps fans differentiate authentic artist merchandise from DIY Noubins made by end-users at home or Blank Noubins. Other creators of Noubins **MUST NOT** use these terms. 
* **Locked NFC Tags:** If you release a Noubin with a non-rewritable NFC tag you MUST use the terms **"Locked" or "Non-Rewritable"** to describe the Noubin somewhere in the descriptive text. 
* **Noubins made from Recycled Materials:** A Noubin made from 90% or greater recycled materials by mass, may be called a **"Re-Noubin"** also stylised as "ReNoubin", "Renoubin" etc. Noubins made from brand new materials MUST NOT use this term. The 90% allows up to 10% new materials to add the NFC chip, support material (e.g. adhesive) and perhaps updated graphics. 
* **Blank Noubins:** When you release a Noubin with a rewritable NFC tag that is not associated with a specific media release (e.g. a visual artist sells 3D printed Noubins to both musical artists for making into official Noubins and to end users for self-writing) than you MUST make this clear by using the term "Blank Noubin" somewhere clearly in the description.
